## DataCite

Bozza paper: https://docs.google.com/document/d/1sCjKj01EmCI6-CdX1uwGllsxma19IpS5VKAfJB8Yny0/edit?usp=sharing

Questione Sara?

* discuss how we can align the work around the SPAR DataCite Ontology and the official DataCite linked-data namespace, without disrupting existing uses or breaking anything that already depends on the SPAR/OpenCitations resources.
* the SPAR ontology can remain maintained and properly credited as the established identifier-focused layer, while the official DataCite namespace can serve as the canonical full-schema reference going forward. To make this work cleanly, I would like to discuss a formal alignment between the two, including mappings between overlapping terms where appropriate.
* one practical issue I would also like to put on the agenda is the future coordination of the datacite: prefix. I would like to discuss how we can handle this in a way that keeps the SPAR/OpenCitations resources stable, preserves existing PURLs, and makes the relationship between the identifier-focused SPAR ontology and the broader official DataCite namespace clear for future users.
* the main question I would like to explore with you is how you see the future role of the SPAR DataCite Ontology. Do you intend to continue maintaining it as a distinct OWL ontology for identifier representation, or would there be interest in aligning it more closely with the official DataCite namespace, so that overlapping parts could potentially be maintained through, mapped to, or superseded by the official representation over time?
* I would also like to better understand what you need from such a representation in practice. As far as I can see, the SPAR ontology uses OWL, while the current DataCite work deliberately takes a lighter RDF/SKOS-based approach that is easier to maintain, map, version, and use operationally. I am trying to avoid introducing a highly constrained OWL hierarchy unless there is a clear use case for it. It would therefore be very useful to understand whether the OWL layer is essential for your current or future use cases, and whether you would need a machine-actionable and reasoner-friendly OWL representation of the full DataCite schema beyond identifiers, or whether formal mappings between the two approaches would be sufficient.
